What is dgem? Architecture & Confidence Beyond Shannon (IDC)

Unlike autoregressive models that generate tokens sequentially (O(N)), dgem places multiple [MASK] decision slots on DiffusionGemma's bidirectional canvas and extracts exact restricted-softmax distributions in O(1) forward passes (~125–400 ms on Cloud Run GPU).

Executive & Product Manager Summary: Why “AI Confidence” Is Hard—And How dgem IDC Makes It Trustworthy
Raw AI confidence scores (like standard single-pass Shannon entropy) suffer from three well-known blindspots: first-option habits (models favor Box A 49%–88% of the time on blank prompts whenever a question is a toss-up), wording/order sensitivity, and missing context (e.g. “Your package could not be delivered” without the sender's email domain). dgem Invariant Decision Calibration (IDC) adds cheap checks around DiffusionGemma's single-pass readout: (1) every choice is bound to a single 1-token letter (A–Z); (2) it “zeros the scale” by dividing out the model's built-in Box A habit (Null-Prior De-Biasing, no labeled data needed); and (3) it can print the ballot forward and reversed on the same canvas, in the same forward pass (Dual-Mirror Canvas), to flag answers whose confidence depends on list order. Early results are promising but small: on a 50-item suite null-prior de-biasing gave 34/34 correct high-confidence answers; the mirror caught one toss-up that a single reading called 99.9% certain (perm_08) and missed another (perm_06). IDC does not address rewording or missing context, and it is CLI-only today.

1. Parallel 1-Token Slot Readout

Evaluates up to 8–12 orthogonal rubric slots simultaneously in a single forward pass. Binds multi-word choices to 1-token letters (A–Z), eliminating multi-token probability decay.

2. Invariant Decision Calibration (IDC)

Goes beyond single-pass Shannon entropy (H̃m) by combining Null-Prior Tare (label-free), an O(1) Dual-Mirror Canvas (reversed-order check in the same pass), and optional T* Scaling (needs labeled data).

3. Wide-Canvas & Bracket Adapter

Eliminates HTTP 422 capacity rejections on K > 26 options (2-Stage Bracket Tournament Routing up to K = 255) and M > 10 slots (Multi-Slot Batching).

EXP-13: Permutation Sensitivity, Null-Prior De-Biasing & O(1) Dual-Mirror Canvas

Why isn't single-pass Shannon entropy (H̃) always sufficient on its own? Because a distribution over option letters measures token-slot confidence under a fixed option order π: z(ℓk | X, π) = s(oπ(k) | X) + bpos(k). When a task is genuinely ambiguous (s(o1) ≈ s(o2)), a strong positional prior bpos(0) toward Slot 'A' inflates whichever option sits in 'A', collapsing single-pass entropy and risking a False Early Exit.

EXP-13B: Content-Free Positional Null Priors (p0)

When dgemma evaluates a content-free state ([REDACTED / CONTENT-FREE CALIBRATION PROBE]), its probabilities are heavily skewed toward Slot 0 ('A'):

Cardinality (K) Slot 'A' Slot 'B' Slot 'C' Slot 'D' 'A' Bias
K = 2 (Binary) 88.3% 11.7% — — 1.77×
K = 3 (3-Way NLI) 78.3% 8.6% 13.1% — 2.35×
K = 4 (4-Way MCQ) 49.3% 4.8% 16.3% 29.5% 1.97×

EXP-13C: O(1) Single-Pass Dual-Mirror Canvas (reads=1)

Because DiffusionGemma denoises all canvas slots in one bidirectional GPU forward pass, dgem places both decision_fwd ([o1 … oK]) and decision_rev ([oK … o1]) on the same diffusion canvas:

  • No extra forward pass (124.7 ms vs 128.8 ms 1-slot, n=16): Both mirrored slots resolve simultaneously in reads=1.
  • One merged answer: a merged forward+reversed output cannot “flip” by construction, so reversal flip rate is not a meaningful metric for it. The two slots can also see each other on the canvas, so they are not independent readings.
  • Catches some false early exits: on perm_08 the single reading said 99.9% (H̃ = 0.007) but forward/reversed readings disagreed (Mirror TVD = 0.258) → escalated. On perm_06 a cyclic reorder flips the answer, yet forward and reversed agree (TVD = 0.0006) → missed. Mean TVD on the 4 ambiguous items is 0.263 vs 0.004 on consensus items, driven by 2 of the 4.

Chronological Execution Order: How dgem + DiffusionGemma Work Together in One Request (~125–300 ms)

For non-ML stakeholders, think of DiffusionGemma as a high-speed judge that reads an entire page of checkboxes at once, and dgem as the ballot designer, digital scale, and traffic controller wrapping that judge in five strict steps:

Step 1 · Pre-Pass (0.1 ms)
Ballot Design & Mirroring

dgem prints the ballot twice on the same canvas: once forward (A..D) and once reversed (D..A), or splits wide lists (>26) into brackets.

Step 2 · GPU Pass (~125 ms)
Diffusion Snapshot Read

DiffusionGemma evaluates all forward and mirrored slots simultaneously in one bidirectional GPU pass and emits raw letter scores.

Step 3 · Post-Pass 1 (0.1 ms)
Tare the Scale (Null-Prior)

dgem subtracts the model's built-in Box A habit (p0 = 49%–88% on blank input), no labeled data needed.

Step 4 · Post-Pass 2 (0.1 ms)
Mirror Merge & Temp Dial

dgem compares the forward & reversed ballots (Mirror TVD) and can apply the Humility Dial (T*, fitted on labeled data) to soften over-sharp scores.

Step 5 · Post-Pass 3 (Gate)
Fast-Exit or Escalate

If uncertainty is low (H̃ < 0.16), dgem returns immediately (66% of the 50-item suite, 33/33 correct); otherwise it escalates to Gemini (98.0% overall, entropy-only gate). A mirror-aware production gate is not implemented yet.

Subject: dgem (DiffusionGemma 26B-A4B) — Unified Readout on JevBench v1.3.1, jev-decision-index & EXP-13 Dual-Mirror Calibration

1. WHAT IS DGEM?
dgem is our zero-shot Decision Model serving, calibration, and policy-routing engine for Google DeepMind's DiffusionGemma (26B-A4B). Instead of generating conversational text token-by-token, dgem evaluates structured multi-slot rubrics (Policy-as-Template) in O(1) bidirectional forward passes (~125-400ms on Cloud Run GPU) and uses per-slot entropy (plus optional IDC order-bias checks) to decide whether to return immediately or escalate ambiguous cases to Gemini.

2. BENCHMARK 1: JevBench v1.3.1 (EXP-11 — Adversarial Rule-Following & 4-Axis Calibration)
• JevBench's 4-axis protocol (Chance-Corrected Intelligence, 10-Bin ECE, Latency, Cost). On the 231-item JevBench set, the upstream DiffusionGemma (djev) reference scores 194/231 (84.0%); the best entropy cascade to Gemini reaches 213/231 (92.2%, 28% escalated). Numbers below use the same protocol on our 50-item calibration suite.
• Slot Temperature Scaling (T* = 1.35): ECE 0.0745 -> 0.0326, but T* was fitted on the same 50 items (in-sample). A held-out test is planned (PROP-01).
• Entropy-Gated Cascade (dgemma -> Gemini Flash, EXP-05b): 66% of items exit at Stage 1 (H_norm < 0.16, 33/33 correct); 34% escalate; 98.0% overall (49/50) at 56% lower cost than Gemini on every item. Entropy-only (no IDC); threshold tuned on the same 50 items.

3. BENCHMARK 2: jev-decision-index (EXP-12 — 5-Area Capability Suite & HF Leaderboard)
• Evaluates 19 panel benchmarks across Knowledge, Language, Retrieval, Tools, and Arts (72,590 cases; capacity rejections score 0.0).
• Why Standard DiffusionGemma Is #2 (55.56) Instead of #1 (55.74) on the Public Leaderboard:
  Inspecting multimodalart/jev-decision-index telemetry reveals DiffusionGemma already beats #1 Jevfire on Knowledge (54 vs 52) and Language (66 vs 63), trailing overall by just 0.18 pts due to three serving bottlenecks in standard open-jev/vLLM engines:
    (a) 26-Option [A-Z] Ceiling: POP909-CL (129 options) collapses to 0.00% (vLLM HTTP 422) / 2.63% (JoshuaSP), and BANKING77 (77 opts) / CLINC150 (151 opts) are rejected.
    (b) Multi-Slot Canvas Overflow: BRIGHT (32 slots/request) drops to 8.48% on vLLM (ContractNLI has 17 slots, ToolRet has 32 slots).
    (c) Unscaled Logit Overconfidence: 75.8% of raw T=1.0 predictions emit 98.8% confidence on 75.2% accuracy, causing 0.2388 ECE.
